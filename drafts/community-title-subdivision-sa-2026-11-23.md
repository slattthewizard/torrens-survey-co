# Community title subdivision in SA: the process and the fees

A community title subdivision splits land into lots, and also creates an area that the lot owners share. That shared area, called common property, is what makes the process different from an ordinary two-block split, and it's where most of the extra paperwork comes from.

This article covers how a community title subdivision works, what the Land Services SA community plan fees are for 2026-27, and which costs nobody can pin down in advance. It's general information, not legal advice. If you want the Torrens-versus-community comparison, we've covered it in a separate post, and this one sticks to the process and the money.

## What a community title is

Land Services SA (LSSA) describes it this way: "A community title is the division of land into at least two lots and an area of common property." The lots are owned separately. The common property is the part everyone shares, such as a driveway, a garden or a bin store.

Two other points from LSSA matter before you go further. First, "By-laws are a compulsory dealings for all community land divisions." You can't create a community title without them, so they're part of the job from the start. Second, new strata divisions are no longer possible in South Australia. If someone offers you one, that's out of date, and community title is the route that exists today.

## Which law it runs under

Land division in South Australia is "the alteration of the boundaries of land", and LSSA says it can be done under Part 19AB of the Real Property Act 1886 or the Community Titles Act 1996. A community title division goes through the second of those.

The planning side runs under the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016, which is separate from the title side. Don't assume one set of fees or one approval covers both, and ask the council or your surveyor which consents your division needs.

## The steps

LSSA lists the sequence for a land division: a preliminary enquiry at the local council, contacting a land surveyor and possibly a conveyancer or solicitor, lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel, lodgement with LSSA, examination of the plan, examination of the dealings, deposit of the plan and issue of title.

For a community title, a few things sit inside those steps:

- The Licensed Surveyor prepares the community plan, which shows the lots and the common property.
- The by-laws are drafted and lodged with the plan. LSSA charges a separate document fee for them.
- A registered conveyancer or solicitor prepares the application, which is "in the form of a legal LSSA dealing".
- The division dealing needs consent from the registered proprietors and from any registered interests on the certificate of title. A mortgage is one of these. Our post on the [certificate of title in SA](/blog/certificate-of-title-sa/) explains how to see what's registered on yours.

LSSA's own timing is "many months, sometimes even years". There's no official month figure.

These are LSSA's plan lodgement fees from 1 July 2026. They change every 1 July, and LSSA doesn't state whether GST applies. "T/F" in the schedule is a $15.00 transaction fee, and it's already included in the two document fees.

## Other fees that apply to community divisions

### The open space contribution

Section 198(2) of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 covers the open space contribution. It applies to a division of land into 20 allotments or less where one or more allotments is under one hectare, and it also covers divisions under the Community Titles Act. The Commission "may" require it, so it isn't automatic.

If it applies in Greater Adelaide, the 2026-27 amount is $10,166.00 for each new allotment that doesn't exceed one hectare. The amount that's charged is the one in force when the application was made. We cover how it works in [open space contribution in SA](/blog/open-space-contribution-sa/).

### Planning fees

The Planning, Development and Infrastructure (Fees) Notice 2026 sets the planning fees from 1 July 2026. Two examples: the certificate of approval fee is $1,229.00, and Commission advice is $240.00. Which fees apply depends on the application, and this article doesn't list them all.

### Conveyancing

Conveyancing Centre, an SA firm, states "$660.00 (including GST) to prepare and lodge" the division forms and "$88 for each consent arranged (including GST)". The page is undated, so check it's still current with whoever you engage. A community title division involves more documents than a simple split, so ask what's covered.

## What a community title subdivision costs overall

Nobody publishes a reliable price for it. No Adelaide surveying firm gives a whole-project figure for a community division, and the surveyor's fee depends on the block, the design and the number of lots. Surveyors quote each job.

The only whole-project figure we've found is for something else. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, said on a page modified 12 May 2025 that a standard one-into-two land division in the Adelaide metropolitan area costs "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000", GST not stated. That's a standard two-lot division, and it's not a community title price. The same firm warns that government costs "increase considerably" when a division creates more than one additional allotment.

So the practical advice is to ask for a written quote that says what it includes. For the wider picture, see the [cost to subdivide land in Adelaide](/blog/cost-to-subdivide-land-adelaide/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the Land Services SA fees for a community plan in 2026-27?

From 1 July 2026, the ACT document fee is $512.00 and the by-laws document fee is $204.00. Examination is $618.00 for five lots or less and $1,231.00 for six or more. Deposit is $189.00, each lot title is $112.00, the common property title has no fee, and the Survey Act Levy is $188.00. These are lodgement fees only, so they're not the cost of the whole subdivision.

### Do I have to pay the open space contribution on a community title division?

It can apply. Section 198(2) of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 covers divisions under the Community Titles Act as well, and the Commission may require the contribution. Whether it does in your case is a question for the council or the Commission, and your surveyor can ask them.

### Are by-laws compulsory for a community title?

Yes. Land Services SA says by-laws are compulsory for all community land divisions. A separate LSSA document fee of $204.00 applies to them for 2026-27.

### Can I create a new strata title instead?

No. Land Services SA states that it's no longer possible to create new strata divisions. Existing schemes can continue, but a new division is either Torrens or community title.
