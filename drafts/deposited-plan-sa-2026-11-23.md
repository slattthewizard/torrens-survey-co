# Deposited plan SA: what deposit means, who lodges it and the 2026-27 fees

A deposited plan is the plan that makes new allotments real. Until it's deposited, a land division is paperwork and approvals. After it, there are separate parcels, and titles can issue for them.

This article covers what deposit means, how the plan gets to Land Services SA, who has to certify it and which 2026-27 fees apply. It's general information, not legal advice.

## What deposit of a plan means

Land Services SA describes deposit as the moment "the allotments are legally created". It's step 7 of its eight-step summary of land division, and it comes just before the last step, issue of title.

That's why lenders care. Land Services SA says "most financial institutions will require the plan to be 'deposited'" before settlements can occur. If you're selling off one of the new allotments, or a lender is financing the build, the deposit is the milestone they wait for.

It's also why a deposited plan isn't the same thing as a plan you get from a boundary job. A boundary identification survey is recorded by lodging a Plan for Information Purposes. The Surveyor-General's guidelines say those plans "are not examined by the LTRO". A deposited plan, by contrast, goes through examination, because it creates parcels. Our articles on [identification surveys](/identification-surveys/) and [land division surveys](/land-division-surveys/) explain the two jobs.

## How a plan reaches deposit

The order of steps matters, because the plan can't be lodged for deposit until the approvals are in. In Land Services SA's list:

1. Lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel
2. Lodgement with Land Services SA
3. Examination of the plan
4. Examination of dealings
5. Deposit of the plan
6. Issue of title

The earlier steps (the council enquiry, the surveyor's work, the planning side) come before this. For the full run, see our guide to [subdividing land in South Australia](/blog/how-to-subdivide-land-south-australia/).

Land Services SA says that once the Certificate of Approval is given, an application together with the plan of division and the necessary consents may be lodged. The plan is lodged by the surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system. The application is a legal LSSA dealing, and a registered conveyancer or solicitor prepares it.

Online lodgement is the normal route. Land Services SA says over 98% of plan types can be lodged online through digital plan lodgement.

## Who has to certify the plan

Under section 47(1) of the Survey Act 1992, "a plan or other document required by law to be signed or certified by a surveyor must be signed or certified by a surveyor who is a natural person". So the certifying surveyor is an individual, not a company and not a website.

The Surveyors Board of South Australia says that only licensed surveyors can undertake a boundary survey or place a survey mark that defines a property boundary. You can check a surveyor's licence with the Registrar on (08) 8212 0343 or at registrar@isasa.org.au, or against the Government Gazette list. Our article on [licensed versus registered surveyors](/blog/licensed-surveyor-vs-registered-surveyor-sa/) explains the difference.

## Consents from registered interests

Plans don't deposit on the owner's signature alone. Land Services SA says the division dealing needs consent "by not only the registered proprietors but also any registered interests on the Certificate of Title".

So before anything is lodged, look at your title. Every registered interest on it is someone whose consent may be needed. Our guide to [reading a certificate of title](/blog/certificate-of-title-sa/) shows where those interests sit. Chase them early, because the dealing needs them.

## Land Services SA plan fees for 2026-27

Land Services SA publishes plan lodgement fees "From 1 July 2026". For a surveyed plan, the schedule lists these fees:

| Fee | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Examination | $1,231.00 | Survey plans |
| Deposit | $189.00 | |
| Each title | $112.00 | Per title |
| Survey Act Levy | $188.00 | Not charged on data plans |

A document fee also applies. The schedule lists two, RTU at $204.00 and RTC at $512.00, both including a $15.00 transaction fee. Land Services SA doesn't say which one a two-lot division uses, so we haven't picked one. The schedule doesn't state a GST treatment either.

We've left out a total on purpose. The per-title fee grows with the number of allotments, and the document fee depends on the dealing. Add them up from LSSA's current schedule, with your surveyor or conveyancer, once the plan is settled.

Data plans have their own line in the schedule: examination $618.00, deposit $189.00 and $112.00 per title, with no Survey Act Levy. Which type of plan your division needs is a question for the surveyor.

One warning. Some third-party pages still show older Land Services SA fees. Use the current schedule, not a page that doesn't say which year it's for.

## Where the plan fees sit in the budget

The deposit and examination fees are part of the Land Services SA step. They're not the whole bill. The planning fees (consent, the land division authorisation, the Commission's advice and the Certificate of Approval fee) sit earlier in the process. Our article on [the cost of subdividing land in Adelaide](/blog/cost-to-subdivide-land-adelaide/) lays them out in order.

For a whole-project figure, one Adelaide firm, Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, said on a page modified 12 May 2025 that a standard one-into-two division is "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000", including the Land Services SA final plan lodgement and examination fees. That's one firm's statement, it doesn't say whether GST is included, and it isn't a quote for your block.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a deposited plan in South Australia?

It's the plan of a land division after it has been examined and deposited at Land Services SA. Deposit is the point at which the allotments are legally created, and titles issue for them afterwards. Most lenders want the plan deposited before settlement.

### Who lodges the plan for deposit?

Land Services SA says the plan is lodged by the surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system, together with an application that a registered conveyancer or solicitor prepares. Over 98% of plan types can be lodged online.

### How much does it cost to deposit a plan in SA?

For a survey plan from 1 July 2026, Land Services SA lists examination at $1,231.00, deposit at $189.00, $112.00 per title and a Survey Act Levy of $188.00, plus a document fee. We haven't totalled them because the document fee and the number of titles vary.

### Does a deposited plan need a Licensed Surveyor?

A plan required by law to be certified by a surveyor must be certified by a surveyor who is a natural person. Only licensed surveyors can undertake a boundary survey or place a survey mark that defines a property boundary, so check the surveyor's licence before you sign.

### Does my mortgage lender have to consent before the plan is deposited?

Land Services SA says the division dealing needs consent from the registered proprietors and from any registered interests on the certificate of title. A registered mortgage is a registered interest, so check your title and talk to your lender early. Your conveyancer or solicitor prepares the dealing and can tell you which consents it needs.
