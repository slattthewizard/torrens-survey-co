# Subdivision conveyancer in Adelaide: what they do and what your surveyor does

Most owners planning a land division start by looking for a surveyor. That's right, but it's half the team. Land Services SA says it's strongly recommended you get a professional licensed surveyor and a conveyancer or solicitor, and the two jobs barely overlap.

This article splits the work between them, explains the paperwork a conveyancer handles and gives the one conveyancing price we could verify. It's general information, not legal advice.

## Two professionals, two different jobs

The short version: the surveyor deals with the land, and the conveyancer deals with the legal paperwork.

| Job | Licensed Surveyor | Conveyancer or solicitor |
|---|---|---|
| Measures and defines the new allotments | Yes | No |
| Draws and certifies the plan | Yes | No |
| Lodges the plan | Yes, through Electronic Plan Lodgement | No |
| Prepares the land division application (a legal dealing) | No | Yes |
| Arranges consents from owners and interests on title | No | Yes |

Land Services SA lists what it strongly recommends professionals do for you: determine the planning and development requirements, draft plans, lodge all applications and necessary paperwork, and monitor the progress of the application. Between them, the two professionals cover that list.

## What the conveyancer does

The fact sheet from Land Services SA (LSSA) says that once the Certificate of Approval is given by the State Commission Assessment Panel, an application together with the plan of division and necessary consents may be lodged with LSSA. The application "is in the form of a legal LSSA dealing", and "A Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor prepares the application."

That application needs consents. LSSA says the division dealing needs consent "by not only the registered proprietors but also any registered interests on the Certificate of Title." Registered proprietors are the owners on the title. A registered interest can be something like a mortgage, so a lender may need to be asked too. Your conveyancer or solicitor will read the title and tell you who.

Gathering those consents is a task for the conveyancer, and it's a good reason to raise the plan early with everyone named on the title. Our article on the [certificate of title](/blog/certificate-of-title-sa/) shows where registered interests appear.

Lenders matter again later. LSSA notes that most financial institutions will require the plan to be "deposited" (when the allotments are legally created) before settlements can occur. If you plan to sell a new lot, that timing is a conveyancing matter as much as a surveying one.

## What the surveyor does

The surveyor's job is the physical and the technical. They work on the plan of division and, under the Survey Act 1992, the plan must be signed or certified by a surveyor who is a natural person (s 47). LSSA says the plan is lodged by the surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system.

Marks matter too. Only a licensed surveyor, or someone working under their supervision, may place a survey mark on land (s 14). For a division into more than 5 allotments, the Survey Regulations 2020 (r 22) require marks to be placed not later than 30 days after completion of works for roads, drains or other services.

## Where each of them sits in the process

LSSA's fact sheet lists the steps in this order:

1. Preliminary enquiry at the local council.
2. Contact a land surveyor and possibly a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor.
3. Lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel.
4. Lodgement with LSSA.
5. Examination of plan.
6. Examination of dealings.
7. Deposit of plan.
8. Issue of title.

The fact sheet predates the 2021 Planning and Design Code, so some of its agency names are old, but the order holds. Step 6, examination of dealings, is the conveyancer's paperwork being checked. Step 5 is the surveyor's plan being checked. They're separate checks, and a problem in either one holds up the deposit of the plan.

One practical point: the fact sheet says "possibly" a conveyancer or solicitor at step 2. LSSA's own page goes further and recommends both. The application has to be prepared by a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor in any case, so plan to engage one.

## What conveyancing costs

Conveyancing is a small slice of the total, and it's hard to find a published number. One SA firm, Conveyancing Centre, advertises that it charges "only $660.00 (including GST) to prepare and lodge any of the above forms and only $88 for each consent arranged (including GST)." Its page is undated, so check that the price still stands, and it's one firm's advertised price, not a market rate.

The same page lists older LSSA fees than the current ones, so don't take any LSSA figure from it. For the current schedule, see LSSA's plan lodgement fees from 1 July 2026.

The bigger picture is a whole-project estimate. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, says a standard one-into-two land division in the Adelaide metropolitan area costs "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000" (page modified 12 May 2025, GST not stated). Its list of what that includes has "Conveyancer or Solicitor Fees", alongside surveyor fees, government fees and SA Water charges.

So you shouldn't add a conveyancing fee on top of that figure. It's already inside. The firm also warns that SA Water non-standard costs "could increase the costs significantly." More on the totals in [the cost to subdivide land in Adelaide](/blog/cost-to-subdivide-land-adelaide/).

## Questions to ask each professional

For the conveyancer:

- Does your quote cover preparing and lodging the application and arranging each consent, or are consents charged separately?
- Who's responsible for dealing with my lender?
- Is GST included in each figure?

For the surveyor:

- Does the quote include the plan, certification and electronic lodgement?
- Who coordinates with the conveyancer so the plan and the dealing match?
- Can you confirm your licence, and which person will sign the plan?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a conveyancer to subdivide land in South Australia?

Land Services SA says the land division application is a legal dealing and that a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor prepares it. It also strongly recommends you get both a licensed surveyor and a conveyancer or solicitor. Ask your conveyancer or solicitor what applies to your division.

### What's the difference between a conveyancer and a surveyor in a land division?

The surveyor deals with the land: the plan of division, its certification and its lodgement through Electronic Plan Lodgement. The conveyancer or solicitor prepares the legal application and arranges the consents of registered proprietors and registered interests on the title.

### How much does a subdivision conveyancer cost?

One SA firm, Conveyancing Centre, advertises $660 including GST to prepare and lodge each form and $88 including GST for each consent arranged, on an undated page. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan's $30,000 to $34,000 Adelaide estimate for a one-into-two division already includes conveyancer or solicitor fees.

### Who lodges the plan with Land Services SA?

The surveyor does. Land Services SA says the plan is lodged by the surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system, and the application and consents that go with it are prepared by a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor.
