# The Greater Adelaide Regional Plan and your plans to split a block

If you've thought about splitting your block, you've probably heard that Adelaide is pushing infill, or that it has stopped. Both claims float around. The 2025 Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) is the document behind them, and it says less about your particular block than people assume.

This article covers what GARP says about infill, what changed from earlier plans, and the gap between a regional plan and a decision about your land. It's general information, not legal advice.

## What the plan is for

GARP is a regional plan. It sets out where growth is expected across Greater Adelaide, a region the State Planning Commission describes as spanning "approximately 11,000 square kilometres from Cape Jervis to Murray Bridge". It's home to around 1.5 million people, approximately 85 per cent of the state's population.

The headline numbers: the Plan identifies the location of 315,000 new homes, for an extra 670,000 people by 2050, plus 254,000 additional jobs.

That's a region-wide planning figure. It isn't a quota for your street, and it doesn't tell you whether your block can be divided.

## The old infill targets are gone

Earlier plans had firm numbers. The 30-Year Plan introduced a target for 70% of new development to occur as infill and 30% as greenfield. The 2017 update went further, aspiring to 85% of all new development in established suburbs.

The 2025 plan has removed them. The Commission says it "has removed the former objective in previous versions which targeted 85 per cent of future housing being infill projects", and the summary states: "Unlike previous plans, a target split for future greenfield and infill development is not required."

So don't repeat the 70% or 85% figures as if they were current. They aren't. If a website, agent or neighbour quotes them, the information is out of date.

## What GARP says about infill now

The summary is blunt on general infill: "No new general infill development areas have been planned or identified for future growth."

It goes on to say that where land already supports this type of development, the focus will be on improving development outcomes to support more tree canopy protection, improved design outcomes, and protecting areas that have character or historic value.

GARP doesn't open up new suburbs for small-scale infill. Where it already happens, the plan's attention is on how well it's done: trees, design and character.

For bigger projects it names specific places. The summary identifies 18 state-led strategic infill sites and 65 locally-led infill investigation areas. If your block sits in or near one, the plan may matter more to you. Even then, the plan doesn't say which individual lots can be split.

## What the housing targets look like by area

GARP groups councils into land supply regions. Each has an annual housing target for 2021 to 2031, and the targets are for the whole region, not one council or street. A few of them:

| Land supply region | Includes | Annual target, 2021 to 2031 |
|---|---|---|
| Inner Metro | Unley, Burnside, Norwood Payneham & St Peters, Walkerville, Prospect, Campbelltown (table also includes City of Adelaide) | 850 |
| Adelaide West | West Torrens, Charles Sturt, western part of Port Adelaide Enfield | 1,450 |
| Inner South | Holdfast Bay, Marion, Mitcham | 790 |
| Adelaide Hills | Adelaide Hills, Mount Barker | 810 |

The plan describes how that growth arrives in each area. The Inner South, for instance, got over 80% of its dwelling growth over the last five years from general infill and "will still be heavily reliant upon general infill to deliver housing." In the west, "recent amendments to tree canopy cover and general infill policy will place a greater reliance on strategic infill development."

## About 90% of the region is closed to new housing

One line in the full plan matters for anyone with a block near the edge of the metropolitan area: approximately 90% of land within the Greater Adelaide region is covered by legislation that prevents new housing. The full plan names the EFPA and the Character Preservation Districts.

Greater Adelaide is big, but the part where housing can be added is much smaller. Whether your land is inside the covered area is something the council or PlanSA can tell you, not the plan's headline figures.

## What GARP doesn't tell you

This is where most owners get stuck. A regional plan sets direction. It doesn't answer the questions that decide a land division.

- **Your zone.** The Planning and Design Code puts each property in a zone and may add overlays. GARP doesn't.
- **Minimum allotment sizes.** They sit in the Code, they differ by zone, and we don't publish a number, because a number from a website is how people buy the wrong block. Our article on [minimum block size and subdivision in Adelaide](/blog/minimum-block-size-subdivision-adelaide/) explains why.
- **Your title and what's on it.** Easements, buildings and existing shared services can change what's possible. See [easements on title](/blog/easements-on-title-sa/).
- **Services.** GARP notes that in the Outer North there's limited housing-ready supply because of a lack of available connections to essential infrastructure, particularly water and wastewater. The same issue can show up at block level.
- **Cost.** A regional plan has no prices. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, puts a standard one-into-two land division in the Adelaide metropolitan area at "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000" (page modified 12 May 2025, GST not stated). That figure bundles government fees, plan fees, surveyor and conveyancer fees and SA Water charges.

For the full cost picture see [the cost to subdivide land in Adelaide](/blog/cost-to-subdivide-land-adelaide/).

## Turning the plan into an answer for your block

Work through it in this order.

1. Ask the council for a preliminary enquiry. That's step one in the Land Services SA fact sheet on the land division process, before any surveyor is engaged.
2. Check your zone and overlays with the council or on PlanSA. Don't rely on a forum post or an agent's guess.
3. Read your certificate of title for easements and registered interests.
4. Ask a Licensed Surveyor whether the block is likely to work and what the survey and plan would involve. Our page on [land division surveys](/land-division-surveys/) explains the survey side.
5. Only then price the whole project.

Land Services SA says it's strongly recommended you get a professional licensed surveyor and a conveyancer or solicitor to determine the planning and development requirements, draft plans and lodge all applications. The same fact sheet says the division of land "can take many months, sometimes even years." GARP doesn't change that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the Greater Adelaide Regional Plan have an infill target?

No. Earlier plans targeted 70% and later 85% of new development as infill, but the 2025 plan removed them. The summary says a target split for future greenfield and infill development is not required.

### Does GARP say I can subdivide my block?

No. GARP is a regional plan. It doesn't say whether any individual block can be divided. That depends on your zone, any overlays and the rules that apply to your land, so ask your council or a Licensed Surveyor.

### How many new homes does the plan identify?

The Plan identifies the location of 315,000 new homes by 2050, for an extra 670,000 people. That's a region-wide figure for Greater Adelaide, not a target for a street or suburb.

### Is the whole Greater Adelaide region open to new housing?

No. The full plan says approximately 90% of land within the Greater Adelaide region is covered by legislation that prevents new housing, through the EFPA and the Character Preservation Districts.
