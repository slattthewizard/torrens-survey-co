# Multi-stage subdivision in Adelaide: what changes once a division gets bigger

A two-lot split and a ten-lot staged division are different jobs. Past a few allotments, or with a new road in the picture, the rules add fees, extra plan requirements and extra duties for the surveyor.

This article goes through the thresholds that the regulations set and what each one triggers. It's general information, not legal advice, and the legislation controls over anything here.

## Stages and the land division certificate

A larger division is often built in stages. The Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 allows the section 138 land division certificate to be given for "a particular stage". A stage can be certified on its own, rather than the whole project waiting for one certificate.

The certificate lapses unless it's extended, and each stage that's certified carries that risk. The Act says the certificate can rest on prescribed conditions being satisfied, or on a binding agreement supported by adequate security. Our guide to [subdividing land in South Australia](/blog/how-to-subdivide-land-south-australia/) shows where it falls in the full process.

How your division is staged is a design decision. It's one to settle with the surveyor before the plan is drawn, not after.

## Where the thresholds sit

Several rules in the General Regulations and the fees notice switch on at set points. This table gathers the ones we've confirmed.

| Trigger | What it brings in | Where |
|---|---|---|
| More than 4 additional allotments, or a public road | Development authorisation fee of $210.00 plus $19.10 per additional allotment (2026-27) | Fees Notice 2026, item 14(b) and (c) |
| More than 5 allotments, or a new road | Contours on the plan, vouched for by a licensed surveyor | General Regs, Sch 8 cl 7(3) |
| Division into more than 5 allotments | Survey marks placed within 30 days after works are complete | Survey Regs 2020, r 22 |
| Road works in the division | Road location and grading plan signed by a licensed surveyor | General Regs, r 85 |
| More than 20 allotments | Council may require up to 12.5% of the land as open space | PDI Act, s 198 |

Each row is covered below. The fees are for 2026-27, and the fee notice doesn't state a GST treatment.

## The planning authorisation fee scales with allotments

For a smaller division, one fee applies. Item 14(a) of the 2026-27 notice sets the development authorisation fee at $210.00 when the division creates no more than four additional allotments and doesn't involve a public road.

Items 14(b) and 14(c) change the formula. Where a division creates more than four additional allotments, or creates a public road, the fee is $210.00 plus $19.10 for each additional allotment created. It's a small amount per lot beside the other fees, but it shows the pattern: the cost rises with the allotment count.

Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, says as much in general terms. On a page modified 12 May 2025 it said government costs "increase considerably for land divisions where it is proposed to create more than one additional allotment". That's the firm's observation, not a fee schedule.

## Contours and the licensed surveyor

For a division into more than five allotments, or one with a new road, Schedule 8 clause 7(3) of the General Regulations says the plan must show "the contours of the present surface of the ground above some known datum level", and that it must "be vouched for by a licensed surveyor as to its reasonable accuracy".

That's a plan requirement, so the contour work is part of the application. It's also a different thing from a development application's feature and level survey. Our article on [contour surveys](/blog/contour-survey-explained/) explains what one records.

## Roads and the surveyor's signature

Where the division includes road works, regulation 85(1) says the works must follow "a road location and grading plan signed by a licensed surveyor and approved by the council".

So a new road puts a second signed document on the surveyor's desk, and a council approval step on yours. If your division needs a public road, ask early what plan the council will want and who prepares it.

## Survey marks after the works

Regulation 22 of the Survey Regulations 2020 deals with the end of a larger division. After a division "into more than 5 allotments", the surveyor must place survey marks "not later than 30 days after completion of works for the provision of roads, drains or other services".

This is a good reason to read quotes carefully. A quote that covers the plan but doesn't mention marking after the works may leave a gap. Only a licensed surveyor can place those marks (Survey Act 1992, s 14), and owners can't move or remove them afterwards.

## Open space in a larger division

The open space rules change with the size of the division. Section 198 of the PDI Act covers a division into 20 allotments or fewer where at least one allotment is under one hectare. The Commission "may" require a contribution. In the 2026-27 fees notice that is $10,166.00 for each new allotment of no more than one hectare in Greater Adelaide, and $3,723.00 elsewhere in the state.

Above 20 allotments the choice is different. The council may require up to 12.5% of the land, in area, as open space instead. We don't have a dollar value for that, because it's land, not a fee.

The amount that applies is the amount in force when the application was made. Our article on the [open space contribution](/blog/open-space-contribution-sa/) goes through who pays and when it can apply.

## What else grows with the project

Some costs don't appear in a table. LSSA says a division of land can take "many months, sometimes even years", and that's before any staging is added. SA Water's non-standard water or sewer work, power connection and the NBN aren't priced in the sources we use, so ask for them separately.

Land Services SA plan fees come on top of the planning fees above. For a surveyed plan, examination is $1,231.00 from 1 July 2026, plus deposit, per-title and levy fees. We haven't added any of it together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a land division be done in stages in South Australia?

Yes. The Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 lets the land division certificate be given for a particular stage. How a particular project is staged is something to agree with the surveyor and the council.

### When does a land division need a contour plan?

Schedule 8 clause 7(3) of the General Regulations requires contours on the plan for a division into more than five allotments or one with a new road. The contours must be vouched for by a licensed surveyor as to their reasonable accuracy.

### What are the survey mark rules after a larger division?

After a division into more than five allotments, regulation 22 of the Survey Regulations 2020 requires the surveyor to place survey marks not later than 30 days after completion of works for roads, drains or other services.

### How much is the open space contribution on a larger division?

For a division into 20 allotments or fewer, the Commission may require $10,166.00 for each new allotment of no more than one hectare in Greater Adelaide in 2026-27. Above 20 allotments, the council may require up to 12.5% of the land as open space instead.

### Does a new road change the fees?

Yes. Item 14(c) of the 2026-27 fees notice sets the authorisation fee at $210.00 plus $19.10 for each additional allotment when the division creates a public road. A road also brings in the contour plan requirement and a road location and grading plan signed by a licensed surveyor.
