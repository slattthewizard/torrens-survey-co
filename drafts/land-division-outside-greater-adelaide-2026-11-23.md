# Rural land division in South Australia: what changes outside Greater Adelaide

Most advice on dividing land in South Australia is written with a suburban block in mind. If your land is in the Riverland, the South East or anywhere else outside Greater Adelaide, some of that advice still holds and some of it doesn't.

This article sorts the two. It's general information, not legal advice. We can tell you the rules that are written down, and the rest has to come from your council and a surveyor.

## What stays the same

The big structure of a land division doesn't care where in the state the land is.

- **Only a licensed surveyor can do the survey.** The Survey Act 1992 says a person must not carry out a cadastral survey for fee or reward unless they're a licensed surveyor or acting under their supervision (s 15), and only a licensed surveyor, or someone they supervise, may place a survey mark (s 14).
- **The plan is certified by a person.** Under s 47, a plan required to be signed or certified by a surveyor must be signed or certified by a surveyor who is a natural person.
- **The steps are the same.** Land Services SA lists them: preliminary enquiry at the local council, contact a land surveyor and possibly a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor, lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel, lodgement with LSSA, examination of the plan, examination of dealings, deposit of plan and issue of title.
- **SA Water is involved in every division.** The General Regulations say the South Australian Water Corporation "is identified in relation to all proposed divisions of land". Its assessment fee is $480.00 for the original assessment and $138.00 for an update, in 2026-27, payable to SA Water by the person applying to divide the land.
- **It's slow.** LSSA says that due to the numerous agencies involved, the division of land "can take many months, sometimes even years." Nothing about a rural address shortens that.

For the full sequence see [how to subdivide land in South Australia](/blog/how-to-subdivide-land-south-australia/).

## What changes: the open space contribution

The clearest difference in the fee schedule is the open space contribution. The Planning, Development and Infrastructure (Fees) Notice 2026, which has effect from 1 July 2026, sets two rates:

| Where the land is | Open space contribution, 2026-27 | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Land within Greater Adelaide | $10,166.00 | each new allotment |
| Any other part of South Australia | $3,723.00 | each new allotment |

The notice words both rates as applying "for each new allotment or strata lot delineated by the relevant plan that does not exceed 1 hectare in area." That wording matters on larger rural land. A new allotment over a hectare isn't described in the fee item, so ask the surveyor or council how your proposed lots would be treated.

Three cautions.

1. **It isn't automatic.** The Commission "may" require the contribution. A 2-lot division creates one new allotment, so the rate in the table is for that one, if the Commission requires it.
2. **The amount in force at the time you apply is the one that counts.** The Fees Regulations say the amount applied "is the amount in force as at the time the relevant application under Part 7 of the Act was made".
3. **It's one fee among many.** Our article on the [open space contribution](/blog/open-space-contribution-sa/) shows where it sits.

We don't total government fees into one figure, because which fees apply depends on the application.

## Why the Greater Adelaide boundary matters

The fee schedule uses the words "land within Greater Adelaide". The Greater Adelaide Regional Plan (GARP) describes that region as spanning approximately 11,000 square kilometres from Cape Jervis to Murray Bridge. That takes in more than the metropolitan suburbs.

GARP also says approximately 90% of land within the Greater Adelaide region is covered by legislation that prevents new housing. So land can sit inside "Greater Adelaide" for the fee and still be in an area where new housing isn't allowed.

We haven't read the rules that apply to every zone outside Greater Adelaide, and we don't want to guess. If your land sits near the region's edge, check which side of the line it falls on before you plan around either fee. The council or PlanSA can tell you.

## What we can't tell you from here

Some answers are specific to your land, and no general article can supply them.

- **Zone and minimum sizes.** We don't publish a minimum block size, for rural land or any other. The zone and any overlays decide it, and the council or a surveyor can check. See [minimum block size and subdivision in Adelaide](/blog/minimum-block-size-subdivision-adelaide/) for why we won't quote a number.
- **Services.** Water, sewer, power and access vary a great deal from town to town and farm to farm. SA Water's own augmentation and connection charges aren't something we could verify.
- **Prices.** We found no regional price estimate for land division, and no survey firm that publishes one. The only whole-project figure we can use is Sawley Lock O'Callaghan's, which is for a standard one-into-two division in the Adelaide metropolitan area: "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000" (page modified 12 May 2025, GST not stated). It isn't a rural figure, and the firm warns that SA Water non-standard costs "could increase the costs significantly."
- **Easements and access.** Check your title for easements before you plan anything. See [easements on title](/blog/easements-on-title-sa/).

### Bigger divisions

If your plan is more than five lots or involves a new road, extra rules apply. For a division into more than 5 allotments, the Survey Regulations 2020 require survey marks within 30 days after works for roads, drains or other services are completed. The plan for a division with more than 5 allotments or a new road must also show the contours of the ground and be vouched for by a licensed surveyor as to its reasonable accuracy.

## How to start

1. Ring the council for a preliminary enquiry. Tell them the address and what you're thinking of.
2. Ask which zone and overlays apply and whether the land counts as inside Greater Adelaide for fee purposes.
3. Read your certificate of title for easements and registered interests.
4. Ask a Licensed Surveyor for a written quote and whether they work in your area. Check the licence with the Registrar on (08) 8212 0343 or at registrar@isasa.org.au.
5. Engage a Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor early, because the application is a legal dealing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the open space contribution cheaper outside Greater Adelaide?

On the 2026-27 fee notice, yes. It's $3,723.00 for each new allotment in any other part of South Australia, against $10,166.00 within Greater Adelaide. The Commission "may" require it, so it isn't charged on every division.

### Does the same surveyor rule apply to rural land?

Yes. Under the Survey Act 1992, only a licensed surveyor, or someone acting under their supervision, may carry out a cadastral survey for fee or reward or place a survey mark. That applies wherever the land is in South Australia.

### What's the minimum lot size for rural land division in SA?

We don't publish a minimum, because it depends on the zone and any overlays that apply to your land. Ask your council or a Licensed Surveyor to check the Planning and Design Code for your address.

### How much does it cost to divide rural land in South Australia?

We couldn't find a regional price estimate. The only whole-project figure we can use is Sawley Lock O'Callaghan's $30,000 to $34,000 for a standard Adelaide metropolitan one-into-two division (GST not stated), which isn't a rural figure. A surveyor and a conveyancer have to quote your job.
