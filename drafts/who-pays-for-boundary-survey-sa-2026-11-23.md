# Who pays for a boundary survey in South Australia?

If you hire a Licensed Surveyor, you pay the bill. That's the default. What changes things is why you're ordering the survey and whether a neighbour has agreed to share it, been served a proper notice, or been ordered by a court to contribute.

This article sets out who ends up paying in the common situations: a fence, a purchase, a build and a land division. It's general information, not legal advice. For a live dispute, talk to the Legal Services Commission, Community Mediation Services or a solicitor.

## The starting rule: whoever orders the survey pays

The surveyor's contract is with the person who engages them. Nobody else owes that surveyor a cent unless they've signed up to it.

SA Government says the same thing about fencing costs in general. By law, you or your neighbour "do not have to contribute to the cost of repairing, maintaining or erecting a fence unless at least one of these apply: you have both agreed to the cost; the proper notices have been given; a court orders payment."

A survey is a different item from a fence, but the same logic carries over. If the other person hasn't agreed, hasn't been served a proper notice and hasn't been ordered by a court, you're carrying the cost yourself.

## When a neighbour can be asked to share

The Fences Act 1975 (SA) is where sharing becomes a legal question. Section 4(1) defines the cost of fencing work to include "the cost of any survey that is reasonably required for the purposes of the fencing work". So a survey can be treated as part of the fencing cost, and that can be put on a notice.

The Legal Services Commission's booklet *Fences and the Law* (December 2023) sets out how it plays out:

- If you both want the survey, you can agree to share the cost.
- If not, the person who arranges the survey pays for it.
- A survey can be included as a cost of the proposed fencing work in a Fences Act notice.
- Your neighbour may object to paying if they believe it isn't necessary.
- If they object and it goes to court, a Magistrate decides whether a survey was reasonably required.

"Reasonably required" cuts both ways. A survey ordered because two owners genuinely disagree about where the line is has a stronger footing than one ordered to win an argument. It also means you shouldn't count on recovering the money. Until a neighbour agrees, or a court rules, the safe assumption is that it's yours.

Our articles on the [Fences Act and boundary fences](/blog/fences-act-sa-boundary-fence/) and [boundary fence dispute surveys](/blog/boundary-fence-dispute-survey-sa/) cover the notice process, including the 30-day window to object.

### What a neighbour's silence means

Under the Fences Act, a neighbour who is served a notice and doesn't object within thirty days is deemed to have agreed to the proposal. The LSC puts it this way: if you receive a Form 1, 2 or 3 and don't object within 30 days, you're legally bound to contribute to the proposal described on the form.

That works in both directions. If you're the one who receives a notice that lists a survey, don't let the 30 days run out while you decide how you feel about it. You don't have to give a reason for an objection.

## Who pays in other situations

Not every survey is about a fence. Here's how the cost usually falls.

| Why the survey is ordered | Who usually pays | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fence dispute | The person who arranges it, unless shared by agreement or a notice | LSC: if you both want it you can share; if not, the arranger pays |
| Buying a property | The buyer, if the buyer orders it | The buyer is the one with the contract |
| Building near a boundary | The owner or builder who needs the boundary offsets | It's their project, so no neighbour has agreed to share it |
| Land division | The owner dividing the land | The owner is the applicant and the client |

### Buyers

The Surveyors Board SA says that when you're buying or selling a house or land you should be aware of any encroachments involving neighbouring properties, and recommends a Licensed Surveyor. If you want that check done before you commit, you're the one asking for it, so you're the one paying. We found nothing that puts the cost on the seller. Whether your contract gives you time to arrange one is a question for your conveyancer or solicitor.

Our article on [identification surveys before buying](/blog/identification-survey-before-buying/) explains what the survey covers.

### Builders and owners planning an extension

The Board says that when building on or near a land boundary, you should engage a Licensed Surveyor to verify your title dimensions and boundary location. The Fences Act speaks about the cost of fencing work. A survey for a build isn't tied to a fence notice, so don't expect a neighbour to chip in unless they choose to.

If a dividing fence is part of the job, ask the surveyor to show the fence-related work separately in the quote. That makes it easier to put on a notice later.

### Land division

A land division is run by the owner, and the surveyor's fee is one cost among many in the project. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, says a standard one-into-two division in the Adelaide metropolitan area is "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000" (page modified 12 May 2025, GST not stated). That figure bundles government fees, plan fees, surveyor and conveyancer fees and SA Water charges. It isn't a surveyor's fee. See [how to subdivide land in South Australia](/blog/how-to-subdivide-land-south-australia/) for the steps.

## What a boundary survey costs (and why we can't give you a number)

None of the Adelaide survey firms we checked publish a price for a boundary survey. They quote each job, and the price depends on the block, the evidence on site and the type of survey.

National guides do exist, and they're a rough guide at best. They aren't Adelaide prices:

- Trade Heroes (updated 6 March 2026) puts a boundary survey at $900 to $2,500, GST not stated.
- EastWest Surveyors (16 November 2025) gives AUD $800 to $2,500 for boundary or identification work, GST not stated.
- Leadkit (15 August 2026) gives $1,200 to $1,800 plus GST for a boundary survey, so ex GST.

Those figures come from marketing blogs, not a market survey. Treat them as a way to spot a quote that looks wildly off, not as a budget. Our page on [land surveying cost in Adelaide](/land-surveying-cost-adelaide/) goes further into why firms won't print a price.

## Settling who pays before the surveyor arrives

Most fights over a survey bill start because nobody said who was paying. A few habits help.

1. Talk to your neighbour before you order. If you both want the survey, put the agreement to share in writing, with the share (half, or some other split) and the cap, if any.
2. Ask for the quote in writing and read what it covers, whether new marks are included and whether GST is in the price.
3. If a neighbour won't agree and you still need the survey, decide whether to go ahead and carry the cost, or use a Fences Act notice and accept that a Magistrate may have to decide if it was reasonably required.
4. If the disagreement is already heated, try Community Mediation Services or the Legal Services Commission before spending anything.

Our [land survey quote checklist](/blog/land-survey-quote-checklist/) lists what to ask the surveyor.

Whoever pays, the survey has to be done by a Licensed Surveyor. The Board says that in South Australia, only licensed surveyors can undertake a boundary survey or place a survey mark that defines a property boundary, and that surveys done by unlicensed practitioners "do not have any legal standing". You can check a surveyor's licence with the Registrar on (08) 8212 0343 or at registrar@isasa.org.au.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who pays for a boundary survey if my neighbour and I disagree about the fence?

The Legal Services Commission says that if you both want the survey, you can agree to share the cost, but if not, the person who arranges it pays. You can include a survey as a cost on a Fences Act notice, but your neighbour can object, and a Magistrate then decides whether it was reasonably required.

### Can my neighbour be made to pay half of a boundary survey?

Not automatically. SA Government says a neighbour doesn't have to contribute unless you've both agreed to the cost, the proper notices have been given or a court orders payment. Even then the Fences Act ties survey costs to fencing work and to a survey that's reasonably required, so get advice if a lot of money rides on it.

### Who pays for a boundary survey when you buy a house?

If you order the survey, you pay. We found nothing that puts the cost on the seller. The Surveyors Board SA recommends a Licensed Surveyor when you're buying, so you know about any encroachments. Ask your conveyancer or solicitor how it fits with your contract and settlement dates.

### How much does a boundary survey cost in South Australia?

Adelaide firms don't publish prices, so a Licensed Surveyor has to quote it. National guides give wide ranges, such as Trade Heroes at $900 to $2,500 (GST not stated) and Leadkit at $1,200 to $1,800 plus GST. Neither is an Adelaide price.
