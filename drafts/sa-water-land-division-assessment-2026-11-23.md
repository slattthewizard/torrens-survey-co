# SA Water land division: the assessment, its fees and what stays unpriced

Every land division in South Australia goes past SA Water. The state's regulations name the South Australian Water Corporation as identified for all proposed divisions, so water and sewer are part of the job from the start, not an afterthought.

This article covers what the assessment is, what it costs in 2026-27, how long it stays valid and which water costs we can't put a number on. It's general information, not legal advice.

## What SA Water does in a land division

Regulation 79 of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure (General) Regulations 2017 says SA Water "is identified in relation to all proposed divisions of land". It may assess what it requires for the division and set that out in an assessment.

That ties back to the Act. Consent for a division under section 102(1)(c) includes "the requirements of a water industry entity" for water and sewerage. So SA Water's requirements aren't optional extras. They sit inside the consent itself.

The assessment is SA Water's statement of what the new allotments need. The quote from a Licensed Surveyor doesn't replace it, and the surveyor doesn't set SA Water's charges.

## The assessment fees for 2026-27

The Planning, Development and Infrastructure (Fees) Notice 2026 took effect on 1 July 2026. Item 27 sets two fees:

| Item | What it covers | Fee (2026-27) |
|---|---|---|
| 27(a) | Original SA Water assessment | $480.00 |
| 27(b) | Update, including where an amended plan of division needs one | $138.00 |

The notice says these fees are payable to the South Australian Water Corporation, by the person who makes the application to divide the land. That's you, as the applicant, not the surveyor. The notice doesn't state a GST treatment, so we don't label the figures.

The update fee is worth noticing. It applies "including where the update is required because of an amended plan of division". If the layout changes after the assessment, the plan changes and the assessment is refreshed for a fee.

## The $480 isn't the end of it

The original assessment fee "must be credited against liability for a fee, charge or other amount set out in the assessment". In plain terms, the $480.00 isn't a separate cost on top of everything else if SA Water's assessment then sets out charges. It's counted toward them.

What those charges are is a different matter. The assessment can set out fees, charges or other amounts, and those depend on the site. We haven't published SA Water's augmentation or connection charges for 2026-27 because the figures weren't available to read when this was researched. Ask SA Water, or ask your surveyor what they've seen on similar sites, and don't rely on a figure from an old page.

## How long the assessment stays valid

An SA Water assessment "is valid for a period of 60 business days after it is delivered to the person who proposes to divide the land". The count starts on delivery, and it's business days, not calendar days.

That window is shorter than it sounds against the whole process. Land Services SA says a division "can take many months, sometimes even years", because of the number of agencies involved. If your assessment runs out before the application is far enough along, you're back at SA Water for an update, and the update fee in the table above is the one that applies.

None of this is a reason to rush the assessment. It's a reason to know where your plan stands before you ask for one. If the layout changes after the assessment is delivered, that can mean an update, so agree with your surveyor what counts as a final layout first.

## Costs outside the application

Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, gives a figure for a standard one-into-two land division in the Adelaide metropolitan area. On a page modified 12 May 2025 it said "in the order of $30,000 - $34,000", including SA Water water and sewage fees. It doesn't say whether GST is included. That's one firm's statement, not a quote for your block.

The same page carries a caveat on water. In Sawley Lock's words, SA Water "may charge non-standard costs for water/sewer extensions, deeper connection pits, or dewatering costs. This could increase the costs significantly."

It adds that connection to power and the NBN are costs "not part of the Land Division Application process". So even a clean SA Water assessment doesn't cover every utility. Power and NBN are separate, and we haven't published SA Power Networks connection charges either.

Land Services SA's fact sheet says the same thing more briefly: other organisations, SA Water among them, "will charge fees for connection of services to the proposed new allotments". ## What to do before you lodge

You can keep the water side from surprising you with a few steps:

1. Ask the surveyor early how water and sewer are likely to reach each new allotment.
2. Ask whether the plan is final. An amended plan can mean an update fee and a refreshed assessment.
3. Read the charges the assessment sets out before you commit, and ask SA Water about anything you don't understand.
4. Keep the 60 business day window in your diary from the day the assessment is delivered.
5. Keep power and NBN on a separate list. They aren't part of the application.

Easements can matter here too, because pipes and drains often run along them. Our article on [easements on title in SA](/blog/easements-on-title-sa/) explains how to find them on your certificate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does SA Water get involved in every land division in South Australia?

Under regulation 79 of the General Regulations, the South Australian Water Corporation is identified in relation to all proposed divisions of land. It may assess its requirements for the division. Whether a particular site needs new mains or sewer work comes out in the assessment.

### How much does an SA Water land division assessment cost in 2026-27?

The original assessment fee is $480.00, and an update is $138.00, including where the update is needed because of an amended plan of division. Both are payable to SA Water by the person who applies to divide the land. The fee notice doesn't state a GST treatment.

### How long is the SA Water assessment valid?

It's valid for 60 business days after it's delivered to the person proposing to divide the land. If it runs out before the application is far enough along, you'd ask SA Water for an update and pay the update fee.

### What will SA Water charge to connect the new block?

We can't tell you, because we haven't published SA Water's 2026-27 augmentation or connection charges. Ask SA Water or your surveyor. One Adelaide firm warns that non-standard water or sewer work could increase the cost significantly.

### Are power and NBN part of the land division application?

No. Sawley Lock O'Callaghan says connection to power and the NBN are costs that aren't part of the Land Division Application process. They're a separate budget line, and a separate set of providers.
