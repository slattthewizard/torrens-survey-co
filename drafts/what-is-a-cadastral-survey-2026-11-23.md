# What is a cadastral survey? A plain guide for Adelaide owners

"Cadastral" turns up on quotes, council letters and surveyors' websites, and it's rarely explained. It's the word the law uses for surveys that determine where land boundaries are. If your job involves pegs on a boundary, a plan of division or a neighbour's fence line, you're probably dealing with one.

This guide covers the legal definition, how a cadastral survey differs from the other surveys you might be offered, the jobs owners most often meet, and who is allowed to do the work. It's general information, not legal advice.

## The definition in the Survey Act

Section 4(1) of the Survey Act 1992 (SA) says a cadastral survey is "any process of determining the boundaries of land by the measurement of distances and angles (including measurement by means of an electronic device) or by photogrammetry".

Two things stand out. The definition is about boundaries, not about buildings, levels or trees. And it's about a process, so it covers the fieldwork and the calculation that follows, not just the pegs at the end.

## Cadastral surveys and other surveys

The Act uses the wider word "survey" for any process of determining the form of land, or the position of a point, object, structure or feature on or in land, by measuring distances and angles. A cadastral survey is one kind of survey. Others measure what's on the ground rather than where the boundary is.

| Survey | What it answers | Cadastral? |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary identification or re-establishment | Where are the existing boundaries? | Yes |
| Land division or boundary realignment | Where will the new boundaries be? | Yes |
| Feature and level or contour | What's on the block, and how does the ground slope? | No, it records features and levels |
| Set-out | Where should something be marked for building? | Ask the surveyor |

That last column comes with a warning. Whether a particular job is cadastral depends on what the surveyor is asked to do, not the name on the quote. If a firm's scope mentions boundaries, ask them to say plainly whether it's a cadastral survey. Our pages on [feature and level surveys](/feature-and-level-surveys/) and the [contour survey guide](/blog/contour-survey-explained/) explain the non-boundary side.

## The cadastral jobs owners actually meet

**Identification or re-establishment.** The surveyor finds where your existing boundaries are and places marks. Under the Survey Regulations 2020, an identification survey is a cadastral survey of existing boundaries of land where no plan is otherwise required to be lodged in the Lands Titles Registration Office. See [identification surveys](/identification-surveys/) and our guide to the [re-establishment survey](/blog/re-establishment-survey/).

**Land division.** Dividing a block into more allotments needs a plan of division. Land Services SA calls land division "the alteration of the boundaries of land", and it recommends a professional licensed surveyor along with a conveyancer or solicitor. Our [land division surveys page](/land-division-surveys/) has the overview.

**Boundary realignment.** Moving a boundary between neighbours is also an alteration of boundaries. The [boundary realignment guide](/blog/boundary-realignment-sa/) covers how that works.

Plenty of other jobs lead here too. A fence dispute, a shed near the line or an extension close to a side boundary can all end with someone saying "we need the boundary pegged".

## Who can carry out a cadastral survey

Section 15 says a person must not carry out a cadastral survey for fee or reward unless they are a licensed surveyor, are acting under a licensed surveyor's supervision, or the survey is carried out through a licensed surveyor or someone supervised by one. A training-course exception also exists. Section 14 applies the same restriction to placing a survey mark.

The Surveyors Board of South Australia explains the two titles. "Licensed Surveyors have specific expertise in the identification and measurement of property boundaries," while "Registered Surveyors have recognised expertise in areas other than land boundary definition", such as engineering, mining and hydrographic surveying. The Board adds: "In South Australia, only licensed surveyors can undertake a boundary survey or place a survey mark that defines a property boundary."

Read [licensed vs registered surveyors](/blog/licensed-surveyor-vs-registered-surveyor-sa/) for more. The check is simple: get the surveyor's name and ask the Registrar, as the Board's [public information page](https://www.surveyorsboardsa.org.au/public-info) explains.

## What you get at the end

For a boundary job, you get survey marks at the corners and a record of what was done. Pegs placed on an identification survey "simply represent the surveyor's opinion as to the position of the boundary and are subject to any errors of survey or judgement which the surveyor may commit", in the words of the Surveyor-General's guidelines. It's a professional opinion backed by measurement, which is why the licence matters.

Under the Board's Code, surveyors publicly record identification surveys by lodging a Plan for Information Purposes. The Land Titles Registration Office doesn't examine those plans, but they're certified as carried out in accord with the Survey Act. For a land division, the end product is a plan of division that goes through examination and deposit, which is a longer process. See our [guide to how to subdivide land in South Australia](/blog/how-to-subdivide-land-south-australia/) for the steps.

Don't move or remove a mark once it's placed. By law you can't, and SA Government says doing so can result in legal action and fines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a cadastral survey the same as a boundary survey?

In everyday use, yes. A boundary survey is a cadastral survey, because the Act defines a cadastral survey as determining the boundaries of land by measuring distances and angles. Surveyors may use "boundary", "identification" or "re-establishment" for the same kind of job, so ask what the scope covers.

### Can a registered surveyor do a cadastral survey?

For a boundary survey or a survey mark that defines a property boundary, the Surveyors Board says only licensed surveyors can do it in South Australia. Registered Surveyors specialise in areas other than land boundary definition, such as engineering, mining and hydrographic surveying.

### Does a feature and level survey show my boundary?

A feature and level survey records what's on the land and its levels. It isn't a substitute for a boundary survey, and SA Government says a property boundary "can only be determined by a licensed surveyor". If boundary position matters to your plans, ask the surveyor whether they'll identify the boundary as part of the job.

### Does every cadastral survey end in a lodged plan?

Not in the same way. Identification surveys are recorded by lodging a Plan for Information Purposes, which isn't examined by the Land Titles Registration Office. Land divisions end in a plan of division that is examined and deposited. Ask the surveyor which applies to your job.
