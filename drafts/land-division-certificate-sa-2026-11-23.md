# Land division certificate SA: what the section 138 certificate is and where it sits

You'll see it called a land division certificate, a section 138 certificate or a Certificate of Approval. They're the same document. It's the point in a land division where the planning side is signed off and the paperwork can move to Land Services SA.

This article explains what the certificate says, what it doesn't, why it can lapse and where it falls in the order of steps. It's general information, not legal advice, and the Act's wording controls over anything here.

## What the land division certificate certifies

The certificate comes from section 138 of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016. The Act calls it a "Land division certificate". In the usual case, it's a certificate from the Commission that it is satisfied the prescribed conditions as to development have been satisfied.

The second situation is a bit different. The applicant can instead enter a binding agreement that is supported by adequate security. That's the route for conditions that can't all be finished before the plan goes in. The security is there so the work still gets done.

Land Services SA calls the same step the Certificate of Approval and says it's given by the State Commission Assessment Panel. So if one document says "certificate" and another says "approval", you're reading about the same milestone.

Two things the certificate is not. It isn't the plan, and it isn't the new titles. It's the sign-off on the development side, and it's what lets the plan and dealings be lodged.

## Where the certificate sits in the land division steps

Land Services SA publishes an eight-step summary of land division. It's an older fact sheet (it still uses "Development Plan" and "ETSA Utilities"), so use it for the order, not the agency names.

1. Preliminary enquiry at the local council
2. Contact a land surveyor, and possibly a registered conveyancer or solicitor
3. Lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel
4. Lodgement with Land Services SA
5. Examination of the plan
6. Examination of dealings
7. Deposit of the plan
8. Issue of title

The certificate belongs at the end of step 3 and the start of step 4. LSSA's wording is that once the Certificate of Approval is given, an application together with the plan of division and the necessary consents may then be lodged with Land Services SA. That's the order that matters: certificate first, lodgement second.

## The certificate can cover a stage

A certificate can be given for "a particular stage" of a land division. That matters on bigger jobs where the division is built in sections. Each stage can have its own certificate.

For a simple one-into-two split there's only one stage, so you can ignore this. For a larger or staged division, it's one of the things to raise early with your surveyor, because it affects how the plan is cut up and when each part is lodged.

## The certificate can lapse

The certificate doesn't last forever. Under the Act it lapses unless it's extended. The source we work from doesn't give a lapse period, so we won't invent one. Ask your surveyor or the council for the current period and the extension process before you rely on a date.

The practical point is simple. Once you hold the certificate, the clock matters. A division that stalls after the planning side is done, because a consent is missing or a conveyancing step drags, can run out the certificate. Then you're asking for an extension, and nobody can promise you'll get one.

## The 2026-27 fee

The Planning, Development and Infrastructure (Fees) Notice 2026 took effect on 1 July 2026. Item 17 sets "A Certificate of Approval Fee for the purposes of Section 138 of the Act" at $1,229.00 for 2026-27. The notice doesn't say whether GST applies, so we don't label it.

It's one fee among several. The same notice has separate fees for the planning consent, the land division authorisation, the Commission's advice and, where it applies, the open space contribution. ## Who does what once you hold the certificate

LSSA says the plan is lodged by the surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system. The application that goes with it "is in the form of a legal LSSA dealing", and a registered conveyancer or solicitor prepares it.

So the people involved at this point are:

| Who | What they do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed Surveyor | Lodges the plan through Electronic Plan Lodgement | LSSA fact sheet |
| Conveyancer or solicitor | Prepares the dealing | LSSA fact sheet |
| Registered proprietors and other registered interests | Give consent | LSSA fact sheet |

The consents point catches people out. The division dealing needs consent from the registered proprietors and from any other registered interests on the certificate of title. Our article on [reading your certificate of title](/blog/certificate-of-title-sa/) shows where those interests are listed.

Under the Survey Act 1992, a plan required by law to be certified by a surveyor must be certified by a surveyor who is a natural person. That's section 47(1), and it's why the plan is a Licensed Surveyor's job, not a firm's or a website's.

## What the certificate doesn't do

It doesn't create the new allotments. In Land Services SA's list, the allotments are legally created when the plan is deposited, after the plan and the dealings have been examined. Most lenders want that deposit before settlement can happen.

It also doesn't make the rest of the process quick. LSSA says the division of land "can take many months, sometimes even years", because of the number of agencies involved. We won't put a month figure on the certificate stage because none is published.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a land division certificate in South Australia?

It's the certificate under section 138 of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016. The Commission gives it when it's satisfied that the prescribed conditions as to development have been satisfied, or when the applicant has entered a binding agreement supported by adequate security. Land Services SA refers to the same step as the Certificate of Approval.

### Is a land division certificate the same as a certificate of title?

No. A certificate of title records who owns a parcel and the interests registered against it. The land division certificate is part of the approval process for dividing that parcel. New titles issue much later, after the plan is deposited.

### How much does the section 138 certificate cost?

The Certificate of Approval fee in the 2026-27 Fees Notice is $1,229.00. The notice doesn't state a GST treatment. Other government fees apply to a division as well, and which ones depends on the application, so don't treat this as the cost of the whole process.

### Can a land division certificate lapse?

Yes. Under the Act the certificate lapses unless it's extended. We haven't published a lapse period because the source we use doesn't give one, so ask your surveyor or the council for the current period and how an extension works.

### Can one certificate cover a staged division?

The Act allows a certificate to be given for a particular stage. On a staged division, each stage can be certified separately. How your project would be staged is a question for the surveyor and the council.
