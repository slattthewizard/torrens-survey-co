Everyone wants a date. You're thinking about selling the back block, or building on it, and the first thing you ask is how long it all takes. The honest answer is that nobody can give you one before they've seen your block, your title and your council.

This article walks through the stages that set the pace, which ones have a published time limit and which don't, and what tends to slow a division down. It's general information, not legal advice.

## What the official source actually says

Land Services SA (LSSA) publishes a fact sheet on the land division process. Its only statement on timing is this: "Due to the numerous agencies involved and the various processes the application must go through, the division of land can take many months, sometimes even years."

That's it. There's no official figure in months for Adelaide, and none for a simple two-lot split. Any number you see online is somebody's estimate, usually from a business that sells something connected to subdividing. Treat it that way.

One example of that kind of claim: Vastu Homes, a builder rather than a surveyor, advertises "Min. 4-6 Months from lodgement of application to get titles" on a page modified 21 Nov 2024. That's a builder's advertising claim. It also starts the clock at lodgement, which leaves out the work before the application goes in. It isn't a promise anyone has made about your block.

## The stages, in order

LSSA lists eight steps: a preliminary enquiry at the local council, contacting a land surveyor (and possibly a conveyancer or solicitor), lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel, lodgement with LSSA, examination of the plan, examination of dealings, deposit of the plan, and issue of title. LSSA's sheet predates the 2021 Planning and Design Code, so some agency names in it are old, but the sequence still holds.

Here's where the time goes, and what's known about each part.

| Stage | Who's involved | What's published about timing |
|---|---|---|
| Council enquiry | Your council | No published figure |
| Survey and plan | Licensed Surveyor | No published figure |
| Planning consent and Commission report | Relevant authority, State Planning Commission | 20 business days (see below) |
| SA Water assessment | SA Water | Valid for 60 business days |
| Land division certificate | State Planning Commission | Can lapse unless extended |
| Lodgement, examination, deposit, titles | LSSA | No published figure |

### Council enquiry and the survey

The preliminary enquiry is a conversation with your council about whether and how your block could be divided. It's not a formal decision. What it tells you depends on the zone and any overlays in the Planning and Design Code, which is why nobody should quote you a block size without checking.

Then there's the survey and the plan. A Licensed Surveyor looks at the title and the ground, and prepares the plan of division. Surveyors quote each job, and no official turnaround time is published for this part.

### Planning consent and the Commission's report

Where a development involves dividing land, the relevant authority can't decide the application until it has received a report from the State Planning Commission. If no report arrives within 20 business days, the authority may presume the Commission "does not desire to make a report" (General Regulations, r 76). That's a limit on how long the authority has to wait, not a promise the whole assessment finishes in 20 business days.

### SA Water

SA Water is involved in every proposed division. Its assessment "is valid for a period of 60 business days after it is delivered to the person who proposes to divide the land" (General Regulations, r 79). That window matters, because if the rest of your application runs past it, the assessment needs updating. The update fee for 2026-27 is $138.00, and the fee schedule says it applies "including where the update is required because of an amended plan of division". The original assessment fee is $480.00.

### The land division certificate

Under section 138 of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016, the Commission can give a certificate that it's satisfied the prescribed conditions have been met. It can be given for a particular stage, and it lapses unless extended. The certificate of approval fee is $1,229.00 for 2026-27.

### Lodgement with LSSA, and titles

Once the certificate is in hand, the plan goes to LSSA with the application and the consents. The surveyor lodges the plan through the electronic plan lodgement system, and a registered conveyancer or solicitor prepares the application. LSSA then examines the plan and the dealings, deposits the plan and issues the titles. LSSA doesn't publish how long any of that takes.

## Why the total is hard to predict

Look back at the table. Most of the stages have no published duration, and the ones that do are limits, not forecasts. Each step also hands the application to a different organisation, and each organisation works to its own queue.

That's what LSSA means by "numerous agencies". The total isn't the sum of eight fixed numbers. It depends on how many questions come back, from whom, and how quickly each one gets answered.

## What tends to slow a division down

Some of these are outside your control. Some aren't.

- **Consents.** The division dealing needs consent from the registered proprietors and from any registered interests on the certificate of title. A mortgage is one of those interests. If a lender takes time to respond, the application waits. LSSA also notes that most financial institutions require the plan to be deposited before settlements can occur, so the lender is involved at both ends.
- **Amended plans.** If the plan changes after SA Water has assessed it, you may need an updated assessment. A plan that's right the first time avoids that step.
- **Unclear title evidence.** If the surveyor can't match the title to what's on the ground, that needs sorting out before the plan is drawn. Our guide to the [certificate of title in SA](/blog/certificate-of-title-sa/) covers what to check.
- **Costs and services.** SA Water may charge non-standard costs for water and sewer extensions, and Sawley Lock O'Callaghan, an Adelaide surveying firm, says those can "increase the costs significantly". Work out what services your block needs early, because it can change the design.
- **Waiting on yourself.** Owners who take weeks to return signed forms or to decide between plan options add that time to the total.

## How to plan around an unknown timeline

You can't fix the date, but you can stop it from hurting you.

Don't promise a settlement, a builder's start date or a loan drawdown on the basis of a figure you found online. If you're selling a lot to fund the build on the front block, talk to your lender early, because of the deposit requirement above.

Ask your Licensed Surveyor to explain, in writing, the stages for your block and which ones are outside their control. A surveyor can't speed up a council or SA Water, but they can tell you what's likely to come back and what you can prepare for in advance.

Ask your conveyancer or solicitor when they'll start preparing the dealing and arranging consents. Consents from other people are one place a division can stall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to subdivide a block in South Australia?

Land Services SA says the division of land "can take many months, sometimes even years". There's no official figure in months for Adelaide, so any number you're given is an estimate. The stages, the agencies involved and the state of your title all affect it.

### Is the 4-6 months I've seen advertised accurate?

It's a builder's advertising claim. Vastu Homes states "Min. 4-6 Months from lodgement of application to get titles", and it counts from lodgement, not from the day you started planning. It's not an official figure, and nobody can confirm it for your block before the application is assessed.

### Are there any official time limits in the process?

Some pieces have published limits. If the State Planning Commission doesn't report within 20 business days, the relevant authority may presume it doesn't wish to report, and an SA Water assessment stays valid for 60 business days. Those are limits on parts of the process, not a guarantee that the whole thing finishes by a set date.

### Can a mortgage delay my subdivision?

It can. A mortgagee is a registered interest on the certificate of title, and the division dealing needs its consent as well as yours. The time a lender takes to respond is outside your control, so talk to them early.
