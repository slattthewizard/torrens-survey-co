A firm's website says "surveyors", the quote looks reasonable and the pegs could go in next week. Before any of that, it's worth a few minutes to confirm that the person doing the work holds a licence. In South Australia the check is free, but it isn't as obvious as typing a name into a search box.

This article walks through how to do it, what the list does and doesn't tell you, and what to ask a firm before you accept a quote. It's general information, not legal advice.

## Why the licence matters

Under the Survey Act 1992 (SA), a person must not carry out a cadastral survey for fee or reward unless they are a licensed surveyor, or are acting under the supervision of one. A cadastral survey is the kind that determines boundaries by measuring distances and angles. Placing a survey mark on land has the same restriction.

The Surveyors Board of South Australia describes the result: "Surveys conducted by unlicensed practitioners do not have any legal standing and may compromise the future development of your property." It also says it has "the power to investigate and prosecute unauthorised people who undertake property boundary surveys."

Get the wrong person and you may pay twice. Our guide to the [difference between licensed and registered surveyors](/blog/licensed-surveyor-vs-registered-surveyor-sa/) covers which type does which work. This article is about the check itself.

## Step 1: get the surveyor's name, not just the firm's

A firm name, a logo or the word "surveyors" in a business name doesn't tell you who will sign off your work. The list you'll check later is a list of names.

Ask for the full name of the individual who will carry out or supervise the survey, and who will certify any plan. The Act says a plan or other document that must be signed or certified by a surveyor has to be signed or certified by "a surveyor who is a natural person". So there's always a named person behind the work. If a firm can't or won't give you one, keep looking.

## Step 2: check the name with the Registrar

The Board says it must maintain a register of Licensed and Registered Surveyors. To check a surveyor, you contact the Registrar:

- by email at registrar@isasa.org.au
- by phone on (08) 8212 0343

Give the surveyor's full name and ask whether they currently hold a licence, as opposed to a registration. The two aren't the same, and only a licensed surveyor can do a boundary survey or place a survey mark that defines a property boundary. The Board's [public information page](https://www.surveyorsboardsa.org.au/public-info) has the contact details and a "Find a Surveyor" section.

There's no searchable online register where you type a name and get a tick. A quick web search won't do the job.

## Step 3: read the Gazette list yourself

Licensed and Registered Surveyors are noted and published in the South Australian Government Gazette. The Board says the list is updated annually in July, after licence renewals, and again in January to add newly Licensed Surveyors. It links to the current list as a PDF from the same public information page.

When you open it, look for these things.

- **The exact name.** Check the spelling and the full name, because surnames repeat.
- **Which list the name is on.** Licensed and Registered Surveyors are listed separately, and you want the licensed one for boundary work.
- **A "Non-Practicing" note.** Some entries are marked "--Non-Practicing". Ask the Registrar what that means for the work you want before you book.
- **The list date.** The list is a snapshot. A surveyor licensed after the last update may not appear yet, which is where the Registrar comes in.

We haven't counted the names and won't quote a figure. The list changes twice a year, and the Registrar is the safer source for anything recent.

## Step 4: remember licences renew every year

A licence or registration "remains in force until 30 June following the grant" and is renewable yearly. So a licence is a current status, not a lifetime badge.

That's why the July update exists. If you're checking around 30 June, or the survey will be signed after that date, ask the Registrar to confirm the position for the time you need it, not just the day you look.

## What to ask a firm before you accept a quote

Ask in writing when you request a quote. Nobody reputable will mind.

| Ask | Why | A good answer |
|---|---|---|
| Who will carry out or supervise the survey? | The list is of people | A full name you can check |
| Will that person certify the plan? | A natural person must sign | A yes, with the same name |
| Will the survey be lodged as a Plan for Information Purposes? | It records the survey publicly | A clear yes or no, and who pays |
| What does the quote cover? | Pegs, a plan and lodging can be priced separately | An itemised written scope |

Our [land survey quote checklist](/blog/land-survey-quote-checklist/) has a longer list. Adelaide firms quote each job and don't publish survey prices, so a written scope is the only way to compare like with like.

## What the check can't tell you

A licence check confirms who is allowed to do the work. It doesn't tell you the price, how busy the surveyor is or how well they communicate. You still need to compare quotes and ask about timing.

It also doesn't make your fence the boundary. SA Government says a property boundary's position "can only be determined by a licensed surveyor", which is why the check matters. If you've already found pegs on your block, leave them where they are. By law you can't move or remove a survey mark made by a licensed surveyor.

If you're in a dispute with a neighbour, a licence check won't settle it. Get advice from the Legal Services Commission, Community Mediation Services or a solicitor.

## What if the surveyor isn't on the list?

Don't book them for boundary work yet. Someone who isn't a licensed surveyor must not hold themselves out as licensed or use the expression "licensed surveyor" to describe themselves. Those offences carry a Division 5 fine, with a maximum of $8,000.

Before you assume the worst, double-check the spelling, ask which name the licence is held under, and then ring the Registrar. If it still doesn't add up, find another firm.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there an online register where I can look up a surveyor?

Not one you can search by name. The Board maintains a register, and you check it by contacting the Registrar by email or phone. The Board also publishes a list of Licensed and Registered Surveyors in the South Australian Government Gazette, updated each July and again in January.

### How often is the list of licensed surveyors updated?

The Board says the Gazette publication is updated annually in July, following licence renewals, and again in January to add newly Licensed Surveyors. Licences themselves run to 30 June each year, so ask the Registrar if you need to confirm a recent change.

### What does "Non-Practicing" next to a name mean for me?

It's a note on the published list, and you should read it as a reason to ask before booking. Check with the Registrar whether that person can do the work you want, and if there's any doubt, choose a surveyor whose entry carries no such note.

### Does checking the firm's name count as checking the surveyor?

No. The Act restricts the expression "licensed surveyor" to people who hold a licence, and the list names individuals. Check the person who will do or supervise the work, not the business name.
