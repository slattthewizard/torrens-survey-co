Sourced facts file. Research date: 30 Sept 2026. Every line below was read on the cited page that day unless a
different date is given. Site positioning: **advertising** that puts Adelaide (South Australia) property owners in
touch with independent Licensed Surveyors, who quote and contract directly with the owner. **The site does not
survey, employs no surveyors, and holds no licence or registration under the Survey Act 1992.** It must never imply
otherwise.

## 0. Legal framing

### 0.A Survey Act 1992 (SA) - statute text

Read from the authorised PDF, "Version: 1.1.2021" (the current version listed on legislation.sa.gov.au on
30 Sept 2026) [S1]. The Act's long title: "An Act to provide for the licensing and registration of surveyors and
to make provisions relating to surveying and land boundaries; and for other purposes." [S1]

Who administers licensing: the Act gives licensing to the "Institution of Surveyors", defined as "the Institution
of Surveyors, Australia, South Australia Division Incorporated". Applications for a licence or registration "must be
made to the Institution of Surveyors" (s 21(1)(a)). [S1] (See 0.B for how the Surveyors Board SA fits in.)

**Definitions, s 4(1)** [S1], verbatim:

- "cadastral survey means any process of determining the boundaries of land by the measurement of distances and
  angles (including measurement by means of an electronic device) or by photogrammetry;"
- "licensed surveyor means a person who holds a licence as a surveyor under Part 3 Division 2;"
- "registered surveyor means a person registered as a surveyor under Part 3 Division 2;"
- "survey means-- (a) a cadastral survey; or (b) any process of determining-- (i) the form of land; or (ii) the
  position of a point, object, structure or feature on or in land, by the measurement of distances and angles
  (including measurement by means of an electronic device) or by photogrammetry;"
- "surveying services provider means a person (not being a surveyor) who provides surveying services through the
  instrumentality of a surveyor;"
- "provide, in relation to surveying services, means provide surveying services personally or through the
  instrumentality of another, and includes offer to provide;"
- "surveyor means a licensed or registered surveyor;"
- s 4(2): "A person who holds a licence under this Act will be taken to be registered under this Act."
- s 4(5): "Without limiting the generality of the expression, a person who is not a surveyor will, unless exempted
  by the regulations, be taken to provide surveying services through the instrumentality of a surveyor if the
  person, in the course of carrying on a business, provides services to the surveyor for which the person is
  entitled to receive a share in the profits or income of the surveyor's practice."
  - **Business-model flag (our reading, not legal advice):** a lead-gen site paid a share of a surveyor's
    profits or income (e.g. a percentage commission) is deemed a "surveying services provider" by s 4(5). A flat
    advertising or per-lead fee is not described by s 4(5). If a revenue share is ever proposed, get legal advice
    first. The only offence Division 3 creates for a provider is s 28 (below).

**s 14 Obligation to be licensed to place survey mark** [S1], verbatim:

> A person must not personally place a survey mark on or in land unless--
> (a) he or she is a licensed surveyor; or
> (b) he or she is acting under the supervision of a licensed surveyor; or
> (c) the survey mark is placed in the course of a survey carried out as part of a course of training approved by
> the Institution of Surveyors.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.

**s 15 Obligation to be licensed to carry out cadastral survey for fee or reward** [S1], verbatim:

> A person must not carry out a cadastral survey for fee or reward unless--
> (a) he or she is a licensed surveyor; or
> (b) he or she is acting under the supervision of a licensed surveyor; or
> (ba) the survey is carried out through the instrumentality of a licensed surveyor or a person acting under the
> supervision of a licensed surveyor; or
> (c) the survey is carried out as part of a course of training approved by the Institution of Surveyors.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.

**s 16 Illegal holding out as being licensed** [S1], verbatim:

> (1) A person who is not a licensed surveyor must not--
> (a) hold himself or herself out as holding a licence under this Act; or
> (b) permit another person to do so.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.
> (2) A person must not hold out another as being licensed under this Act unless the other person is a licensed
> surveyor.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.

**s 17 Illegal holding out as being registered** [S1], verbatim:

> (1) A person who is not a registered surveyor must not hold himself or herself out as being registered under this
> Act or permit another person to do so.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.
> (2) A person must not hold out another as being registered under this Act unless the other person is a registered
> surveyor.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.

**s 18 Limitation on use of certain expressions** [S1], verbatim:

> (1) A person who is not a licensed surveyor must not use the expression "licensed surveyor" or any of its
> derivatives, or any expression reserved in the regulations for use by licensed surveyors, to describe himself or
> herself.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.
> (2) A person who is not a registered surveyor must not use the expression "registered surveyor" or any of its
> derivatives, or any expression reserved in the regulations for use by registered surveyors, to describe himself or
> herself.
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.
> (3) A person must not, in the course of advertising or promoting a service that the person provides, use an
> expression reserved in this section or the regulations for use by licensed or registered surveyors, or any
> derivative of such an expression, to describe a person who is engaged in the provision of the service but who is
> not licensed or registered under this Act (as the case may require).
> Penalty: Division 5 fine.
> (4) The regulations may exempt (conditionally or unconditionally) persons of a specified class from the provisions,
> or any specified provisions, of this section.

- **Reserved expressions in the regulations: none found.** The Survey Regulations 2020 (Version 15.1.2026) have no
  regulation reserving any further expression; their contents are Part 1 Preliminary, Part 2 Licences and
  registrations (rr 4-8), Part 3 Survey instructions (rr 9-26), Part 4 Code of Ethics (r 27). [S2] So on the
  statute text the reserved expressions are only "licensed surveyor" and "registered surveyor" and their
  derivatives. **Neither the Act nor the Regulations restricts the bare words "survey" or "surveyor" in a business
  name** (nothing found; this is an absence-of-text finding, not a ruling). Business-name registration rules
  (ASIC) were not checked.

**s 20 Disqualification** [S1], verbatim: "A court finding a person guilty of an offence against this Division may,
in addition to imposing a fine, order that the person be disqualified from being licensed or registered under this
Act permanently, for a specified period, until fulfilment of stipulated conditions or until further order."

**s 28 Improper directions etc to surveyor by surveying services provider** [S1], verbatim:

> (1) If a person who provides surveying services through the instrumentality of a surveyor directs or pressures the
> surveyor to act unlawfully, improperly, negligently or unfairly in relation to the provision of surveying services,
> the person is guilty of an offence.
> Penalty: Division 4 fine.

**s 47 Certification of plans** [S1], verbatim: "(1) A plan or other document required by law to be signed or
certified by a surveyor must be signed or certified by a surveyor who is a natural person."

**s 46 Right to enter** [S1], verbatim: "A surveyor, or a person authorised in writing by a surveyor, may-- (a) at any
reasonable time, enter any land for the purposes of carrying out work in connection with a survey; and (b) take such
action as is necessary to enable the survey to be carried out effectively (including excavating or breaking the
surface of land to the extent necessary to uncover or place a survey mark)."

**s 52 is headed "Interference with survey marks"** (text not transcribed here). [S1]

**Penalty amounts** (Appendix to the Act, "provided for convenience of reference only", as at the version date,
per s 28A Acts Interpretation Act 1915) [S1]:

| Division | Maximum imprisonment | Maximum fine |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 years | $15 000 |
| 5 | 4 years | $8 000 |

- So s 16 (and ss 14, 15, 17, 18) carry a **Division 5 fine: maximum $8 000**. The Act states "Division 5 fine", so
  the fine applies, not the imprisonment column. [S1]

**Licensing requirements (Survey Regulations 2020)** [S2]:

- r 5 qualifications include "a Graduate Diploma in Surveying from the University of South Australia", "a Master of
  Surveying" and "a Bachelor of Geoinformatics and Surveying" (UniSA), "a Bachelor of Engineering (Honours)
  (Surveying)" (UniSA), "a Bachelor of Geospatial Information Systems/Bachelor of Surveying from Flinders University",
  or accredited equivalents.
- r 6: a new licence applicant "must have a total of at least 400 days of practical experience in surveying including
  at least 200 days practical experience in cadastral surveying"; a registration applicant needs "at least 400 days
  of practical experience in surveying".
- r 17: a surveyor carrying out an identification survey "must comply with the provisions of the Lodgement of
  Boundary Identification Surveys Code". "identification survey means a cadastral survey of existing boundaries of
  land for which a plan is not required by law (other than by this regulation) to be lodged in the Lands Titles
  Registration Office". The Code is "the Code of Practice--Lodgement of Boundary Identification Surveys, prepared by
  the Surveyors Board of South Australia, 17 March 2016".
- r 22: after a division "into more than 5 allotments" the surveyor must place survey marks "not later than 30 days
  after completion of works for the provision of roads, drains or other services".
- s 24 of the Act: a licence or registration "remains in force until 30 June following the grant" and is renewable
  yearly. [S1]

## 1. Survey types and when an SA owner needs each

Government sources describe the legal categories (cadastral / identification / land division). Plain-English
descriptions of feature, level and set-out surveys come from SA firms and are marked as such.

### 1.1 Boundary identification / re-establishment ("re-pegging")

- Legal definition: "identification survey means a cadastral survey of existing boundaries of land for which a plan
  is not required by law (other than by this regulation) to be lodged in the Lands Titles Registration Office".
  Surveyors doing one "must comply with the provisions of the Lodgement of Boundary Identification Surveys Code".
  (Survey Regulations 2020 r 17) [S2]
- Because it is a cadastral survey done for a fee, only a licensed surveyor (or someone under their supervision) may
  do it (Act s 15), and only they may place the boundary pegs (s 14). [S1]
- Surveyor-General on what the pegs mean: pegs placed on identification surveys "simply represent the surveyor's
  opinion as to the position of the boundary and are subject to any errors of survey or judgement which the surveyor
  may commit" (quoting Foxall). [S9, Cadastral Survey Guidelines V6.0, 29 Sept 2023, p 32]
- The Code has surveyors "publicly recording their identification surveys through lodging a Plan for Information
  Purposes". "While Plans for Information Purposes are not examined by the LTRO, they are certified as having been
  carried out in accord with the Survey Act 1992". [S9, s 4.12]
- When owners need one (government wording): if "you and your neighbour don't agree with the boundary location"
  [S6]; to "confirm if an encroachment has occurred" [S7]; "When building on or near a land boundary" [S4]; when
  "buying or selling your house or land", to "be aware of any encroachments involving your neighbouring properties"
  [S4].

### 1.2 Building encroachment / check before purchase

- "An encroachment is when a structure intrudes onto another person's land, crossing the legal boundary. It can
  include buildings, driveways, eaves and balconies." "Hire a licensed surveyor to mark the legal boundary to confirm
  if an encroachment has occurred." Encroachment disputes go to the "Supreme Court". [S7]
- Surveyor-General: "S.4 of the Encroachments Act 1944 confers upon both the adjacent owner and an encroaching owner
  the right to apply to the Land and Valuation Court for relief." [S9, s 4.15]
- Board: "A boundary dispute can result in expensive litigation, particularly if major improvements you are
  undertaking need to be removed or relocated." [S4]

### 1.3 Feature (detail) and level / contour survey for a Development Application

- PlanSA pages could not be read (Cloudflare bot check on plan.sa.gov.au; not bypassed). The binding list of what a
  DA must include is in Schedule 8 of the Planning, Development and Infrastructure (General) Regulations 2017, read
  on legislation.sa.gov.au (PDF "Version: 17.9.2026"). [S10]
- Sch 8 cl 2 (planning consent for new buildings, structures or extensions) requires "a site plan, drawn to scale"
  showing among other things "the boundaries and dimensions of the site", "the minimum distance between any proposed
  building and the front, side and rear boundaries of the site" and "existing ground and floor levels (if relevant),
  and proposed finished floor levels and proposed site (or "bench") levels, including in relation to the top of any
  kerb level, showing the height and location of any earthworks or retaining walls (if relevant)". [S10]
  - **The regulation does not say who must prepare that site plan.** Do not say "a licensed surveyor is required
    for a DA site plan". Safe wording: "a feature and level survey is the usual way to get the existing levels and
    boundary offsets a site plan must show" (practice statement; see firm description below).
- Land division with more than 5 allotments or a new road: the plan must show "the contours of the present surface of
  the ground above some known datum level ..." and "be vouched for by a licensed surveyor as to its reasonable
  accuracy." (Sch 8 cl 7(3)) [S10]
- Land division plans generally must show "all relevant topographic features", "all existing registered easements"
  and "the position of any buildings intended to be retained on the land and the approximate position of any buildings
  which are to be demolished or removed" (Sch 8 cl 7(2)(a)). [S10] Clause 7 "does not apply with respect to a division
  of land which is deemed-to-satisfy development"; for those "Only a "final plan" is required". [S10]
- Firm description (City Surveyors Adelaide, low-reliability source, see 4.4): "a detail survey of your property
  helps inform planning decisions". [S21] Use only as general colour.

### 1.4 Set-out survey

- Regulation link: road works in a land division must follow "a road location and grading plan signed by a licensed
  surveyor and approved by the council" (General Regs r 85(1)). [S10]
- No government page defining a building set-out survey for SA homeowners was found. **Blank** for a definition;
  firm wording only if added later.

### 1.5 Land division surveys (Torrens, community, strata)

- Land Services SA: "Land division is the alteration of the boundaries of land and can be done under Part 19AB of the
  Real Property Act 1886 or the Community Titles Act 1996. Plans of division relating to the Strata Titles Act 1988
  or the Community Titles Act 1996 can be amended." [S11]
- It "is strongly recommended you get a professional licensed surveyor and a conveyancer or solicitor to: determine
  the planning and development requirements; draft plans; lodge all applications and necessary paperwork; monitor the
  progress of the application". [S11]
- Title types (LSSA fact sheet, PDF created 1 June 2021) [S12]:
  - "Torrens title land division is the division of land into at least two allotments, which are held independently of
    one another. There are generally no shared facilities or infrastructure."
  - "A community title is the division of land into at least two lots and an area of common property."
  - "In a community strata scheme there must be at least one lot that exists above another and the lot boundaries must
    be defined by reference to parts of the building."
  - "It is no longer possible to create new strata divisions but existing strata schemes can continue and are regulated
    under the Strata Titles Act 1988." **Never offer a new "strata title" subdivision.**
  - "By-laws are a compulsory dealings for all community land divisions."
- Planning pathway for simple divisions: an "Accredited professional--surveyor may act as a relevant authority for the
  purposes of giving planning consent and consent under section 102(1)(c) or (d) of the Act in relation to development
  that is constituted solely by the division of 1 or more allotments and that may be assessed as deemed-to-satisfy
  development ... other than where there are 1 or more minor variations ... or where it is necessary in connection with
  the division for land to be vested in another entity or a public road to be created" (General Regs r 22). [S10]
- Plans: "A plan or other document required by law to be signed or certified by a surveyor must be signed or certified
  by a surveyor who is a natural person." (Survey Act s 47(1)) [S1]

## 2. Land division process and government fees (SA)

### 2.1 Steps

LSSA's fact-sheet summary (it predates the 2021 Planning and Design Code: it still says "Development Plan", "ETSA
Utilities" and "Transport SA"; use the step list, not those names) [S12]:

> 1. Preliminary enquiry at Local Council
> 2. Contact Land Surveyor and possibly Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor.
> 3. Lodgement and approval from the State Commission Assessment Panel
> 4. Lodgement with LSSA
> 5. Examination of Plan
> 6. Examination of Dealings
> 7. Deposit of Plan
> 8. Issue of Title

Current statute detail to use alongside it:

- Consents: a land division needs planning consent and, "in relation to a proposed division of land (otherwise than
  under the Community Titles Act 1996 or the Strata Titles Act 1988)", consent against the s 102(1)(c) requirements,
  which include the Code, "the requirements of a water industry entity" for water and sewerage, and s 198 open space.
  (PDI Act s 102(1)) [S13]
- Commission advice: where a development "involves the division of land, the relevant authority must not ... make a
  decision on the application until it has received a report from the Commission". If none arrives "within 20 business
  days", it may presume the Commission "does not desire to make a report". (General Regs r 76) [S10]
- SA Water: "the South Australian Water Corporation ... is identified in relation to all proposed divisions of land"
  and may assess its requirements; an assessment "is valid for a period of 60 business days after it is delivered to
  the person who proposes to divide the land". The original assessment fee "must be credited against liability for a
  fee, charge or other amount set out in the assessment". (General Regs r 79) [S10]
- Certificate: s 138 "Land division certificate": "a certificate from the Commission that it is satisfied that the
  prescribed conditions as to development have been satisfied", or that the applicant has entered a binding agreement
  "supported by adequate security". It may be given for "a particular stage". It lapses unless extended. [S13]
- LSSA: "Once the Certificate of Approval is given by the State Commission Assessment Panel, an application together
  with the plan of division and necessary consents may then be lodged with Land Services SA. The plan is lodged by the
  surveyor through the Electronic Plan Lodgement system." The application "is in the form of a legal LSSA dealing" and
  "A Registered Conveyancer or Solicitor prepares the application." [S12]
- Consents on title: the division dealing needs consent "by not only the registered proprietors but also any
  registered interests on the Certificate of Title". [S12]
- Lenders: "most financial institutions will require the plan to be "deposited" (when the allotments are legally
  created) before settlements can occur." [S12]
- Online: "Over 98% of plan types can be lodged online through digital plan lodgement". [S11]

### 2.2 Timeline (official wording only)

- LSSA: "Due to the numerous agencies involved and the various processes the application must go through, the division

## 3. Fences and boundaries

### 3.1 Fences Act 1975 (SA), statute text [S17, Version 22.6.2023]

- "court means the Magistrates Court". (s 4(1))
- **Survey cost is a fencing cost**: "cost in relation to fencing work-- (a) includes the cost of any survey that is
  reasonably required for the purposes of the fencing work; and (b) includes the cost of any work reasonably required
  to facilitate the performance of the fencing work; and (c) where an adjoining owner has done, or proposes to do, any
  of the work personally, includes a reasonable allowance for his labour". (s 4(1))
- "dividing fence means a fence dividing contiguous land of adjoining owners". Land is contiguous if there is "a
  common boundary" or it is "separated from the other land by a right of way of no more than one metre in width".
  (s 4)
- **s 5 Notice of intention (new fence).** "The notice must be in the Form No 1 in the Schedule to this Act and must
  state-- (a) the length and position of the proposed fence; (b) the nature of the proposed fence; (c) an estimate of
  the cost of the erection of the proposed fence; (d) the amount that the proponent seeks to recover from the adjoining
  owner towards the cost of the proposed fence; (e) where the proposed line of the fence encroaches into the land of
  the adjoining owner, whether the proponent proposes to pay compensation to the adjoining owner for loss of
  occupation, and, if so, the amount of that compensation; (f) the name and address of any contractor or other person
  by whom the proposed fence is to be erected." (s 5(2))
- Repair, replacement or maintenance uses "Form No 2" (s 5(4)). The cross-notice is "Form No 3" (s 6(2)).
- **s 6 Cross-notice:** an objecting neighbour "may, within thirty days after the service of the notice, serve a
  cross-notice". "it shall not be necessary to assign any reason for an objection". (s 6(1), (4))
- **s 7:** a person who does not object in time "shall be deemed to have agreed to the proposal or counter-proposal".
- **s 8:** work may proceed "after the expiration of thirty days from the date of service of the notice, if he is not
  served with a cross-notice during that period", or after agreement or a court decision. No contribution is
  recoverable for work done before that. (s 8(1)-(2))
- **Cost sharing, s 12(6)-(7):** disputes over shares are decided "according to the benefit that each of the adjoining
  owners derives", and "in the absence of proof to the contrary it shall be presumed that the adjoining owners derive
  equal benefit". For an adequate or better fence, absent agreement, the neighbour's contribution "shall be one-half
  of the minimum cost of erecting an adequate fence". An adequate fence (non-rural case) "conforms with general
  standards of good fencing existing in the locality ... and is adequate for the purposes of the owner against whom
  contribution is sought". (s 12(8)(a))
- **Court powers, s 12(2):** the Magistrates Court may decide "the line of fence to be adopted", compensation "for loss
  of occupation of land as a result of the erection of a fence otherwise than upon the boundary", and may "order the
  removal of a fence or any portion of a fence not erected upon the proper boundary". (s 12(2)(a), (h))
- **s 17:** where a dividing fence is built off the boundary under an agreement or court order, "neither of the
  adjoining owners shall, by reason of his occupation of the land enclosed by the fence, be deemed to be in adverse
  possession of any land of the other so as to acquire title to that land".
- Minor civil: the Act refers to "determining a matter in a minor civil action under this Act" (s 23(4)); procedure
  is under the Magistrates Court Act 1991 (s 24).
- The Schedule forms are images in the PDF and were not transcribed. SA Gov publishes the notices as Word files:
  "Notice_of_intention_to_erect_a_fence.doc" and "Notice_of_intention_to_replace_repair_maintain_a_fence.doc". [S6]

### 3.2 SA Government and Legal Services Commission

- SA Gov: "The fence's existing location does not change: the legal boundary; who legally owns the land; who owns the
  fence." "Your fence is jointly owned by you and your neighbour". "The boundary can only be determined by a licensed
  surveyor." [S6]
- SA Gov: "Your neighbour has 30 days to give you a cross-notice ... if they object to your proposal. You cannot start
  work until the 30 days have passed or you have received written notice from them consenting to the work." [S6]
- SA Gov: "By law, you or your neighbour do not have to contribute to the cost of repairing, maintaining or erecting a
  fence unless at least one of these apply: you have both agreed to the cost; the proper notices have been given; a

- LSC *Fences and the Law* (dated "December 2023") [S18]:
  - "if there is a dispute about where a fence should go, get a survey done which will show the precise location of

    fencing work. However, your neighbour may object to paying for a survey if they believe it is not necessary. If

  - "a court is unlikely to order the relocation of a fence if the deviation from the boundary is minor."
  - "If you receive a Form 1, 2 or 3 and do not object within 30 days, you will be legally bound to contribute to the

  - Court: "either neighbour can apply to the local Magistrates Court"; "the application can be lodged online through
